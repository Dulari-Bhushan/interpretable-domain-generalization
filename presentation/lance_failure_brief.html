Failure-mode brief · CBMs for Continual DG

Why LanCE won't survive continual domain generalization

Zeng et al., "Explaining Domain Shifts in Language: Concept Erasing for Interpretable Image Classification" (LanCE), CVPR 2025 — arXiv:2503.18483

LanCE erases domain-specific concepts using a domain-descriptor set the LLM writes exactly once, before training. Every mechanism that makes it work in single-shot domain generalization is the same mechanism that makes it structurally unable to handle domains arriving over time — now measured, not just argued: 8 experiment phases across two testbeds (PACS, Office-Home) and one out-of-distribution modality (EuroSAT).

Five failure modes

Strong = grounded in the paper's own numbers/admissions. Confirmed = we ran the experiment ourselves. Moderate = architecturally clear, not paper-tested and not run this round.

FM·1 — §4.3, Eq. 13

Closed-world domain-descriptor set

Strong
Mechanism

200 domain descriptors, generated by one GPT-3.5 prompt before training, never revisited. DDO loss only erases influence from domains representable in this fixed set.

Continual consequence

Any domain arriving after t=0 that wasn't anticipated gets zero orthogonality protection — no discovery or update mechanism exists.

Own ablation, Table 2/8: domain-irrelevant descriptors underperform relevant ones — LADV-3D OOD 65.46 (irrelevant) vs 68.01 (relevant). Benefit is coverage-dependent, not general.
Our extension (Phase A) came back null: accuracy stayed flat (55.9–57.1%) even stripping the descriptor pool to the 24 least-similar entries. Doesn't disprove closed-world — relocates it to FM·3's "wrong modality" level instead of "wrong style within a covered family."
FM·2 — §4.1, Eq. 14

No incremental update path

Confirmed
Mechanism

L = L_CE + λ·L_DDO is solved once, jointly, over D_train. No update rule is defined for after deployment.

Continual consequence

Adding a domain needs D_train again to retrain from scratch — often unavailable under continual-learning constraints. Fine-tuning W_F instead risks catastrophic forgetting of earlier domains.

Corroborated by the literature: CONCIL and CI-CBM had to build dedicated anti-forgetting machinery (recursive regression updates, pseudo-concepts) for exactly this failure in the same model family — LanCE has no analog.
Phases B–D (PACS → Office-Home): weak/order-dependent on PACS (BWT −8.3 in 1 of 3 orderings, ceiling-masked); real and consistent on Office-Home (BWT −0.7 to −4.7 in all 3 orderings). Cheap fixes (cumulative DDO, replay) nearly solved PACS's forgetting but only partly close Office-Home's — the best-evidenced result in the whole project.
FM·3 — §3, App. G

Frozen CLIP breaks the core premise outside its pretraining distribution

Strong
Mechanism

The whole method rests on CLIP aligning visual and textual domain shifts — validated only on domains dense in CLIP's web-scraped pretraining data (sketch, painting, sculpture, clipart, 3D-render). All 200 stock descriptors are conventional art/media styles; zero scientific or industrial modalities appear.

Continual consequence

Continual DG must eventually meet domains the frozen backbone predates (post-2021 diffusion-art styles) or domains scarce in web image-text pairs (medical, remote-sensing, thermal). This isn't fixable by adding descriptors — the shared embedding space itself is the weak point.

The authors admit it directly — Appendix G, verbatim: "these models are limited in application to some professional fields like medical treatments." Their proposed fix is one-off fine-tuning, not a continual mechanism.
Phase F1 (EuroSAT): photo→satellite alignment scores 0.32 — vs. the paper's own 0.90–0.99 range, no overlap. Phase F2 refines why: a trained CBM reaches 90.9% (near the 98.1% linear-probe ceiling, not the ~60% zero-shot ceiling we predicted) — the visual representation is fine, it's specifically zero-shot alignment that's broken, and DDO's text-only mechanism has no way around that.
FM·4 — §4.1

Static concept bank, fixed output layer

Moderate
Mechanism

Concept set C and the M → N_y linear layer W_F are both fixed size, chosen once before training.

Continual consequence

New concepts or classes arriving with new domains can't be absorbed without architecture surgery — which reopens FM·2's forgetting problem.

CI-CBM frames "concept-incremental and class-incremental learning" as its own dedicated research task specifically because "existing CBMs typically assume static datasets."
Not tested this round — deliberately deferred (mixing class-arrival with domain-arrival would blur which axis causes what in Phases B–D's forgetting numbers). Still architectural, not empirical.
FM·5 — §5.3

Structural gaps persist even at full coverage

Strong
Mechanism

2D→3D shift is explicitly in the descriptor list ("3D model," "CGI render," "low-poly") — LanCE's best case, not an uncovered domain.

Continual consequence

If a fully-anticipated shift still leaves a wide gap, expect degradation to compound for domains with zero descriptor coverage and zero CLIP exposure — this corroborates FM·1 and FM·3, not a standalone claim.

Paper's own numbers, LADV-3D: 99.93% ID vs 68.01% OOD, even with DDO applied. Authors: "generalization from 2D to 3D remains challenging."
Not independently re-tested, but corroborated in spirit: Phase D showed a harder benchmark reveals gaps PACS's easier task hid, and Phase F1's 0.32 alignment gap is far larger than anything the paper reports even for its own worst case.

Literature gap map

Checked against 6 papers spanning the adjacent clusters — none occupy the intersection this research targets.

Cluster Representative work What it solves Why it's not this
CBM + continual learning Lang.-Guided CBM for CL (CVPR'25) · CONCIL · CI-CBM New classes/concepts over time, within one domain No domain shift at all
CBM + domain generalization LanCE (this paper) · PCBM · LaBO Unseen domains, single-shot train-once No streaming / sequential aspect
CBM + temporal drift Tree of Concepts Non-stationary tabular clinical data over time Not visual; one drifting distribution, not discrete domains
Continual domain-incremental DGCL (Oct 2025) Sequential domains, generalize across all encountered Black-box — no interpretability, no language-guided descriptors
Concept-based adaptation CUDA (ICML'25) Aligning concepts using target-domain data Adaptation, not generalizing to future unseen domains

The intersection of concept-based interpretability, generalization to unseen future domains, continual arrival without forgetting, and dynamic descriptor updating is unclaimed — a defensible white space, not an assumed one.

What we verified

The four predictions above, now run — 8 experiment phases, PACS + Office-Home + EuroSAT, traced to results/.

01
Coverage-gap test — null result

Phase A: predicted accuracy would collapse as the descriptor pool got less similar to the true domain. It stayed flat (55.9–57.1%) even stripped to the 24 least-similar descriptors. Reprioritized the closed-world critique toward FM·3, not confirmed at this level.

02
Out-of-CLIP-distribution test — confirmed, with a refinement

Phase F1: photo→satellite alignment scored 0.32 vs. the paper's 0.90–0.99 range — large-margin confirmation. Phase F2: a trained CBM hit 90.9%, not the ~60% we predicted — it's zero-shot alignment specifically that's broken, not the visual backbone, and DDO inherits exactly that weakness by design.

03
Sequential-domain forgetting test — confirmed, scaling with difficulty

Phases B–D: weak and order-dependent on PACS (1 of 3 orderings), real and consistent on Office-Home (3 of 3, BWT −0.7 to −4.7). The clearest, best-evidenced result of the project — real forgetting, moderate not catastrophic, and only partly fixed by textbook remediations on the harder benchmark.

04
Descriptor-set staleness test — not run

Freeze P at GPT-3.5-era knowledge; test against a domain a newer LLM would name but GPT-3.5 wouldn't. Out of scope for this round — still an open prediction, flagged honestly rather than dropped silently.
